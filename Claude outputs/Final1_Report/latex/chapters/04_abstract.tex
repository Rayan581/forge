\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Most procedural content generators build levels for an average player, and most dynamic difficulty systems in games simply scale enemy health or damage up and down. Neither fits the individual player well. \textbf{FORGE} is a closed-loop adaptive dungeon generator for a 2D top-down combat roguelike built in Godot 4.7. After every floor, the game turns the player's results (clearing time, deaths and damage taken), the current difficulty settings and the floor number into an 8-dimensional observation. A Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) policy maps this observation to an 8-dimensional action that sets enemy health, damage, speed and aggro radius, hazard density, resource scarcity, room count and the mix of five enemy types. The policy is trained offline in Python and runs inside the game in plain GDScript, with no external runtime. Floors 1 to 5 are fixed calibration floors that give the policy its first observation.

For the FYP-1 Final evaluation the committee asked us to learn the difficulty Judge from data and explain it, and to show a benefit over a strong non-adaptive baseline. This report describes a revised architecture with four parts: the PPO generator, a learned player model (MLP1) that will replace the hand-written player simulator, a learned Judge that predicts how hard a floor will feel, and Policy SHAP, which explains the generator's own decisions.

We are clear about what is finished. Implemented and tested: five enemy archetypes, SQLite telemetry with a consent screen and a 1--5 difficulty rating after each floor, a retrained policy (simulator v3) that beats a random policy, a rule-based heuristic and a static $+8\%$ baseline on all six simulated player profiles, and an offline LightGBM Judge with TreeSHAP. On held-out synthetic players the Judge has a mean absolute error of $0.052$, against $0.104$ for the old hand-weighted formula. On the only real data we have (29 floors from one player) it does \emph{not} beat that formula, which we report as a sim-to-real gap. Still to be built for Final-1: the player model, the calibrated simulator, the PPO retrain on it, Policy SHAP and the BSP baseline generator. All player-based results in this report are pilots, because they rest on one player. A 10-participant user study in FYP-2 will test whether FORGE improves the player's experience.
